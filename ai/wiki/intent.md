# Intent

**Read this before Current State, every session.** This page says what
the game must *become*. [[po-rules]] says what must not break.
[[requirements-ledger]] lists discrete asks. None of those three is this.

## Who owns this

The PO holds **taste and veto**. He does not hold a plan: the game began
as a test of GPT's capabilities and continued because he liked the result.
*"Il n'y a pas réellement de vision poussée pour le jeu de mon côté"*
(2026-08-20). The stated goal is an **homage to Universal Paperclips, and
the best game of that kind** — with the honest precondition that we do not
yet know what makes a good one.

So **the direction is mine to propose and to defend with research**, his
to refuse. He never reads this page; every line here must be sayable to
him aloud, in player terms, in one sentence. See
[[005-direction-before-queue]].

## 1. The player should feel smart

Not "the player should have choices". The feeling he named is **mastery**:
working something out, then using it on purpose and watching it pay.

> *"people want to feel smart and feel like they have made a choice other
> players may not have done. Like they are different (even if they are
> not, you get what I mean...)"* — `claude_transcript.txt:1114`

The parenthesis is the whole design. **He is explicitly authorising the
illusion.** The difference between players may be small; the *feeling* of
having taken a distinct path may not be.

Two ways to serve it, and they are different psychological needs:

- **Competence — primary.** A player notices that two systems interact,
  works out the rule nobody stated, does it deliberately, and is paid. He
  says *"j'ai compris le truc du sucre"*.
- **Autonomy — secondary, later.** Two players finish different runs and
  compare. He says *"j'ai fait la version montagne"*.

Decided 2026-08-20: **competence first, autonomy later.** Both are
established drivers of enjoyment (SDT — Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006);
**no evidence ranks them** for a single-player game with no social layer,
so the ranking is a product call, not a finding ([[research-queue]] H5).
It stands on two things: the competence loop builds on systems that
already exist and work, and the loudest live complaint — *"I don't
understand anything in Act II, just pushing buttons"* — is a competence
failure, not a choice failure.

## 2. There is always something to do, decide, or understand

> *"On ne veut pas un jeu plan plan où tu n'as quasiment rien à faire et
> juste les chiffres montent, ce n'est pas du tout gratifiant pour le
> joueur, ce n'est pas fun."* — 2026-08-20

The axis is **engaged ↔ bored**, not relaxed ↔ stressful. Engagement
produces a light pressure — something can be missed — and that is
healthy, provided it is visible coming, actionable and survivable.

Our pressure comes from the jam, not from a slot machine: jam catches if
you leave it, the setting point passes, fruit spoils, market day ends. A
visible clock, an available gesture, a recoverable failure.

Worth knowing, since Paperclips is the homage target: **it does not get
calmer, it gets more demanding.** Critics reading it closely argue the
later game stops being idle at all and grows more attention-hungry as it
goes ([[research-queue]] H6). The PO's instinct that a good game applies
some pressure is what the target actually does.

Without stakes, a good decision feels like nothing. **Pressure is what
makes being smart feel like anything at all.**

## 3. Every action feels like something — and good ones feel different

> *"Sentir une satisfaction quand tu fais une action plutôt qu'une autre.
> Satisfaction de voir les chiffres monter."* — 2026-08-20

Two halves, and the second is the one we fail:

**Feedback on every action.** Near-universal practitioner consensus —
though checked on 2026-08-20 and found to rest on craft literature and one
famous GDC demo, not on controlled measurement ([[research-queue]] H3).
Do it anyway; just do not call it evidence. See [[juice-and-legibility]].

**Differentiated feedback.** If everything is juicy, nothing is. A good
decision must feel *different* from a mediocre one, not louder. Today
every button gives the same tier or none, so the game rewards **activity,
not judgement** — which is why Act II reads as button-pushing.

And the rising number has a half-life. Every shipped game in the genre
uses exponential curves and they converge on a narrow band (≈1.07–1.15 per
step). How fast the sensation actually decays is **unmeasured anywhere** —
open question 2 in [[research-queue]]. Re-anchor — a new resource, a new
scale, a new panel — rather than adding zeroes.

*your words* · differentiation and decay `confirmed` 2026-08-20

## 4. The automation must still feel like the hand

> *"they would really like to 'stir the pot', kinda like cookie clicker
> again... and then the auto things get the pot stirring automatically but
> still satisfyingly"* — `gpt_transcript:1604`

Automation is not the removal of the gesture. It is the gesture,
continuing without you. Right now automation is silent — numbers change
and nothing happens ([[gap-idle-player]]).

*your words*

## 5. A weakness always buys a strength; nothing is ever unwinnable

> *"there is no 'wrong', there is just tradeoffs... deal with it like in
> the real world and make your next choices smartly to accommodate your
> new weakness (and potentially the strength you gained alongside)"*
> — `gpt_transcript:648`

And the fork must be **perceptible**: after choosing, the player can name
something that changed on screen within a few minutes — but cannot compute
that the other branch was strictly better. Small divergence, large
perceptibility. See po-rules 10.

## 7. Fully French, written by a French writer

Not a direction so much as a scar: nine separate complaints about English
surviving a "fully translated" claim, and specific corrections showing a
real ear — *"agitation doesn't work in french... une plainte d'épaule
doesn't either, it would be more une douleur constante à l'épaule, see the
difference?"* (`gpt_transcript:1369`). Humour must survive translation
without crossing into cringe; he named that failure mode before it
happened, and it happened.

*your words* · enforced as po-rule 6

## What he reaches for, in his own words

Kept because a ledger row destroys them.

- **He plays his own game and does not understand parts of it.** *"I would
  also, because I'm not clear on that [taste]"* (`gpt:1616`). Said almost
  apologetically. It is a legibility bug report wearing a disclaimer.
- **Tactility matters to him as much as systems.** *"the jam price in the
  first phase is very tiring to move, cent by cent and not able to stay
  pressed, you need to make this more satisfying"* (`gpt:1592`). Framed in
  feeling — "tiring", "satisfying" — never in mechanics.
- **He wants skill-gating preserved, not removed.** On the blind tasting
  exploit: *"maybe this should be a feature only rewarding the players that
  make the effort to try understand the math of it"* (`gpt:1611`). He
  rejected the exploit, not the system. Worth remembering before nerfing
  anything: he is protective of the reward for effort.
- **He borrows shapes, not themes.** The sugar dial came from *"a park
  tycoon game where you managed the salt in fries to get people to buy
  water as well"* (`gpt:1623`) — a dial with a hidden inflection point. The
  comparison target never matters; the property he is pointing at does.
- **He hedges his diagnosis and hands the decision back.** *"I don't know
  at this point if tuning this further would actually undo the balance of
  the game... you tell me"* (`gpt:817`). Recurring: he proposes, then
  defers. Those are invitations to decide, not instructions to obey.
- **He asked for the research on day one.** *"if you don't do a little
  psychology research on game reward and keeping people engaged in..."*
  (`claude:1119`). Nobody did it. He asked again on 2026-08-20, in more
  detail. Twice is not a preference, it is a requirement.
